Logbook · Paris, January 1349
The Plague killed a third of the city in six months. I walk through half-empty streets. The bodies are still warm. But something strange is happening: the survivors who worked yesterday for almost nothing are suddenly demanding double. The lords are furious. They call it ingratitude. I call it economics.
Economic note Before the Plague: 1 agricultural day-labourer ≈ 1 denier per day. After (1380): 3–4 deniers. The death of 30–50% of Europe's population between 1347 and 1353 created a labour scarcity that the Ordinances of 1351 (France) and the Statute of Labourers (England) tried — in vain — to block by law.
Even a catastrophe can reduce inequalities — if it redistributes the balance of power between workers and property owners. Inequality is never inevitable.

How inequality is perceived

The feudal order produces a radically different justification from Antiquity: inequality is no longer natural — it is theological. God willed the three orders — those who pray, those who fight, those who work — and to question this is to question divine will.

Thomas Aquinas takes up Aristotle but integrates him into a Christian framework: earthly hierarchy reflects celestial order. Inequality is providentially useful.

Women’s Thread — Act II

Hildegard of Bingen (1098–1179): abbess, composer, naturalist, theologian. Her case illustrates the medieval paradox: the convent as a space of intellectual autonomy for elite women, but a space entirely dependent on male ecclesiastical authority. The exception proves the rule: gender inequality is as ontological as status inequality.

The data — The Black Death effect

Show code 
df <- tibble::tribble(
  ~period,                     ~gini, ~year,
  "Europe, before Plague (1300)", 0.44,  1300,
  "Black Death peak (1350)",      0.38,  1350,
  "Recovery (1400)",              0.41,  1400,
  "Late Middle Ages (1450)",      0.43,  1450
) |> dplyr::mutate(period = forcats::fct_reorder(period, year))

p <- ggplot2::ggplot(df, ggplot2::aes(
  x = year, y = gini, group = 1,
  text = paste0(period, "<br>Gini: ", gini)
)) +
  ggplot2::geom_line(color = "#8B2020", linewidth = 1) +
  ggplot2::geom_point(color = "#8B2020", size = 3) +
  ggplot2::geom_vline(xintercept = 1347, linetype = "dashed", color = "#C8962A", linewidth = .5) +
  ggplot2::annotate("text", x = 1349, y = .435, label = "Black Death", hjust = 0,
                     size = 3, color = "#C8962A") +
  ggplot2::scale_y_continuous(limits = c(.35, .48)) +
  ggplot2::labs(x = NULL, y = "Estimated Gini (Europe)",
                caption = "Source: Milanovic & al. (2011), estimates for Western Europe") +
  ggplot2::theme_minimal(base_size = 11, base_family = "serif") +
  ggplot2::theme(panel.grid.minor = ggplot2::element_blank())

plotly::ggplotly(p, tooltip = "text")
