What Act III left open
Adam Smith claimed that the invisible hand transforms individual self-interest into collective good. But he himself acknowledged that the division of labour degrades the worker. Act IV shows how Ricardo, Marx and their contemporaries transform this tension into a theory of conflict.

Smoke, noise, children working from the age of six. A man handed me the freshly printed Communist Manifesto and explained that the capitalist legally robs the worker.
Economic note Textile worker weekly wage: 7 shillings. Rent: 2s. Food: 4s. Left over: 1 shilling. A mill director earns £15/week — 60 times more.
Industrial inequality is the first to be frontally challenged as a system, not an accident.

How inequality is perceived

In the 19th century, inequality ceases to be invisible. It piles up in cities, pours out of chimneys, is documented by parliamentary inquiries, novels and nascent statistics. And for the first time, it is frontally challenged as a system.

Marx does not say poverty is sad — he says it is structurally produced by capitalism. This changes entirely what political response is possible.

How it is justified and contested

Thinker Position Key_argument
Ricardo Justifies Iron law of wages — tend toward subsistence minimum
Malthus Justifies Poverty inevitable — population grows faster than resources
Marx Contests Surplus value = legalised exploitation; capital is stolen labour
Engels Contests Fieldwork inquiry (Condition of the Working Class, 1845)
Mill Reforms Tempered liberalism — redistribution possible without destroying liberty
Proudhon Contests ‘Property is theft’ — economic anarchism

Women’s Thread — Act IV

Rosa Luxemburg (1871–1919): the first woman to produce a major theoretical work in Marxist economics. The Accumulation of Capital (1913) anticipates Keynes on effective demand and grounds imperialism in economic necessity. Beatrice Potter Webb (1858–1943): co-founder of the London School of Economics, pioneer of fieldwork on working conditions. Her research on cooperatives and trade unions shapes British labourism.

Note

Connection to Act V
Marx predicted the collapse of capitalism through its internal contradictions. In the 20th century, it does not collapse — it is regulated. Keynes, the welfare state and trade unions produce the compression of inequalities of the Glorious Thirty. Act V shows how and why.
