Logbook · Athens, 350 BCE
I arrived in a city of 300,000 souls, of whom 100,000 are slaves. No one counts them in the assemblies. The philosopher I met this evening is called Aristotle. He explains that the slave is an "animated instrument" — a tool that speaks. When I ask him whether the tool suffers, he changes the subject.
Economic note The price of a skilled slave: 3 to 5 silver minae. The daily wage of a free craftsman: 1 drachma (1/100 mina). The slave pays for himself in 300 days of work. After that, pure profit. The first economic question is not how is wealth produced but who has the right to benefit from it.
Ancient inequality is not a malfunction — it is the foundation of the system. Understanding this changes everything about reading Aristotle.

How inequality is perceived

Ancient inequality is ontological: it precedes economics. It is not the result of a system — it is the condition of the system.

Aristotle distinguishes oikonomia (rational household management) from chrematistics (unlimited accumulation), which he condemns as unnatural. A founding paradox: the same philosophy justifies slavery and criticises greed.

Women’s Thread — Act I

Aspasia of Miletus (~470–400 BCE): companion of Pericles, influential rhetorician, excluded from citizenship despite her central intellectual role in Athens. Her case illustrates how the regime of ontological inequality applies simultaneously to slaves and free women. Aristotle does not include women among beings capable of full political reason.

The data

Show code 
df <- tibble::tribble(
  ~society,             ~gini, ~year,
  "Classical Athens",    0.42,  -400,
  "Republican Rome",     0.43,  -100,
  "Imperial Rome",       0.49,   100,
  "Roman Egypt",         0.45,   200,
  "Byzantium",           0.41,   600
) |> dplyr::mutate(society = forcats::fct_reorder(society, year))

p <- ggplot2::ggplot(df, ggplot2::aes(
  x = society, y = gini,
  text = paste0(society, "<br>Estimated Gini: ", gini, "<br>ca. ",
                ifelse(year < 0, paste0(abs(year), " BCE"), paste0(year, " CE")))
)) +
  ggplot2::geom_col(fill = "#8B2020", alpha = .75, width = .6) +
  ggplot2::geom_hline(yintercept = .40, linetype = "dashed",
                       color = "#C8962A", linewidth = .5) +
  ggplot2::annotate("text", x = .7, y = .41,
                     label = "extraction threshold (Milanovic)", hjust = 0,
                     size = 3, color = "#C8962A") +
  ggplot2::scale_y_continuous(limits = c(0, .65)) +
  ggplot2::labs(x = NULL, y = "Estimated Gini",
                caption = "Source: Milanovic, Lindert & Williamson (2011)") +
  ggplot2::theme_minimal(base_size = 11, base_family = "serif") +
  ggplot2::theme(panel.grid.minor = ggplot2::element_blank(),
                  panel.grid.major.x = ggplot2::element_blank())

plotly::ggplotly(p, tooltip = "text")

Further reading — Act I
Essential
Aristotle — Politics, Book I (trans. C.D.C. Reeve, Hackett)
Read chapters 4–7 on natural slavery in particular. Short, dense, indispensable.
Essential
