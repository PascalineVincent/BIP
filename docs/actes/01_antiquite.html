Logbook · Athens, 350 BCE
I arrived in a city of 300,000 souls, of whom 100,000 are slaves. The philosopher Aristotle explains that the slave is an "animated instrument" — a tool that speaks. When I ask whether the tool suffers, he changes the subject.
Economic note The price of a skilled slave: 3–5 minae. A free craftsman earns 1 drachma (1/100 mina) per day — the slave pays for himself in 300 days.
Ancient inequality is not a malfunction — it is the foundation of the system.

How inequality is perceived

Ancient inequality is ontological: it precedes economics. It is not the result of a system — it is the condition of the system.

Aristotle distinguishes oikonomia (rational household management) from chrematistics (unlimited accumulation), which he condemns as unnatural. A founding paradox: the same philosophy justifies slavery and criticises greed.

How it is justified

Thinker Justification Blind spot
Aristotle Natural hierarchies The slave as ‘animated instrument’
Plato Myth of the metals Equality reserved for the guardians
Epicurus Voluntary frugality Addresses only free men
Xenophon Efficient household management Women as resource, not as subjects

Women’s Thread — Act I

Aspasia of Miletus (~470–400 BCE): companion of Pericles, influential rhetorician, excluded from citizenship despite her central intellectual role in Athens. Her case illustrates how the regime of ontological inequality applies simultaneously to slaves and free women. Aristotle does not include women among beings capable of full political reason.

Suggested readings

Further reading — Act I
Essential
Aristotle — Politics, Book I (trans. C.D.C. Reeve, Hackett)
Read chapters 4–7 on natural slavery in particular. Short, dense, indispensable.
Essential
Milanovic B. — Global Inequality (2016), Appendix A
Method for estimating historical Gini coefficients. Accessible and rigorous.
In-depth
Moses Finley — The Ancient Economy (1973, University of California Press)
The classic thesis: the ancient economy cannot be read through modern categories.
Culture
Spartacus — Stanley Kubrick (1960)
Historical fiction on the slave revolt. Watch with a critical eye on Hollywood reconstruction.
Data
wid.world — World Inequality Database
