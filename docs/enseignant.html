Teacher Guide

Pedagogical uses · Adaptations · Assessment

For whom, for what

This site is intended for students from Year 2 undergraduate to Masters in economics, history, social sciences or political philosophy. It can be used in three ways.

🖥️
Self-directed pathway
Students follow the 6 acts at their own pace, complete quizzes and fill in the logbook. The XP system and Thomas ensure progression. Duration: 8 to 10 hours.
🏫
Hybrid (online + in-person)
Acts are read independently. In-person time is used for structured debates and logbook sharing. See suggested sessions below.
📋
Tutorial material
Each act can be used as a tutorial document: chart to comment on, group quiz, dilemma to debate. Each act is self-contained.
🎯
Formative assessment
The logbook (12 guided entries) can serve as a portfolio assessment. Export via the Journal page and paste into a Word document.

Levels and adaptations

Audience Recommended pathway Duration
Introductory (Year 1–2) Acts I–III + global quiz + 1 debate 5–6h
Intermediate (Year 3) 6 acts + detective + data workshop 8–9h
Advanced (Masters) Full pathway + full logbook + all debates 12h+

Suggested in-person sessions

Session 1 — Launching the course1h30
Run the diagnostic quiz collectively (5 min, results aggregated anonymously on the board). Introduce Thomas and the XP system. Read Act I journal together (10 min). Oral debate: "Is inequality natural or constructed?" — without prior teaching, to surface initial representations. Materials Projector + diagnostic quiz open on each workstation.
Session 2 — Data and method1h30
Data workshop in pairs: each pair analyses one chart (Global Gini, Kuznets, r > g) and presents their observations in 3 minutes. Plenary: what are the limits of the Gini as an indicator? Extension: look up France's Gini on WID.world and compare with the Act V chart. Materials Data Workshop page + access to wid.world.
Session 3 — Structured debate1h30
Use Debate II (efficiency vs equity) or Debate III (capital taxation). Groups of 4: 2 defend position A, 2 defend position B — even if it is not their conviction. 15 min preparation, 15 min debate, 10 min debrief. Students can then revise their position in the logbook. Materials Structured Debates page + board for key arguments.
Session 4 — Logbook restitution1h
Each student shares one logbook entry of their choice (2 min). Identify convergences and divergences in ideological profiles (My Progress page). Close with the final question of Act VI: "What is to be done?" Materials Logbook export (copy-paste) + My Progress page for profile.

Assessment

The logbook (12 entries) forms a natural formative assessment portfolio. Suggested criteria:

  • Mobilisation of authors — do answers cite at least one course thinker with their specific argument?
  • Use of data — are claims supported by orders of magnitude or course charts?
  • Argued position — does the student defend a position or merely summarise?
  • Evolution across acts — do later answers show more nuanced thinking than earlier ones?
Tip

Logbook export
The Logbook page allows each student to export all their entries as a copyable text block. They can paste it into a Word or PDF document to submit it.

Adaptation and error reporting

The full source code is available in the deployment ZIP. Each .qmd file can be modified to adapt content, add acts, change logbook questions or quiz questions.

To report a factual error or suggest an improvement, use the About page.
