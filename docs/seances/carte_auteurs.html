How to read the map

  • Rows are schools of thought; a figure sits in the row of the school where the course places them. Some people (Samuelson, Robinson) belong to several conversations: the lines show where.
  • Influence lines are drawn only where the course states or implies a link (teacher and pupil, reception, formalisation, a title that honours another author). Criticism lines mark explicit debates (Say vs Malthus, Pigou vs Keynes, Friedman vs the Phillips curve, Hayek vs Keynes). Contrast lines connect rival approaches that are best read side by side.
  • Gold dots are the women economists and the women’s-thread figures of the platform. They are present in every period, though often connected through collaboration or editing rather than through the schools.
  • Use the idea tags to follow a theme across time, for example Distribution and inequality from Ricardo to Piketty, or Demand and crises from Sismondi to Keynes and HANK.
