Glossary

65 terms from the course · searchable

Accelerator
Investment depends on past income growth (Hansen–Samuelson oscillator).
Ch. 10
Animal spirits
Keynes: confidence and instinct behind investment decisions.
Ch. 9
Balassa–Samuelson effect
Catching-up countries see systematically rising prices.
Ch. 10
Bullionism
Mercantilist monetary doctrine: wealth is the stock of precious metals.
Ch. 2
Child penalty
Earnings loss women experience after childbirth; main cause of today’s gender gap.
Ch. 12
Chrematistics
Art of accumulating money; Aristotle condemns its mercantile form as unnatural.
Ch. 1
Commons
Ostrom: resources managed by communities through self-organised rules.
Ch. 12
Consumer surplus
Dupuit, Marshall: gap between what a buyer would pay and what they pay.
Ch. 7
Creative destruction
Schumpeter: innovation drives growth and crises.
Ch. 9
Division of labour
Smith: specialisation that raises productivity (the pin factory).
Ch. 4
DSGE
Dynamic stochastic general equilibrium models used by central banks.
Ch. 9
Edgeworth box
Diagram of exchange between two agents and two goods.
Ch. 8
Effective demand
Keynes: demand determines output and employment.
Ch. 9
Embodied vs commanded labour
Smith’s two conceptions of value; Ricardo keeps the embodied one.
Ch. 4
Endogenous growth
Romer: ideas and knowledge have increasing returns.
Ch. 12
General equilibrium
Walras: all markets are solved simultaneously.
Ch. 7
Gold standard
Convertibility of currencies into gold at fixed parities (1870–1914, interwar).
Ch. 9
Greedy jobs
Goldin: highly paid jobs that demand total availability, driving residual gender pay gaps.
Ch. 12
HANK
Heterogeneous-agent New Keynesian models; the multiplier depends on distribution.
Ch. 11
Historical materialism
Marx: material conditions of production shape society and ideas.
Ch. 6
HOS theorem
Countries export goods using their abundant factor.
Ch. 10
Human capital
Becker: knowledge, skills and health accumulated by individuals.
Ch. 12
IS-LM
Hicks (1937): goods-market and money-market equilibrium.
Ch. 9
Just price
Aristotle: buyer free in choice, seller not in a position of strength; Aquinas: both feel better off.
Ch. 1
Laissez faire
Principle that the state should not obstruct the natural order of the economy.
Ch. 3
Liquidity preference
Keynes: demand for money, which sets the interest rate.
Ch. 9
Liquidity trap
Rates near zero make monetary policy ineffective.
Ch. 9
Lucas critique
Estimated parameters change when policy changes.
Ch. 11
Marginal utility
Satisfaction from one more unit; basis of subjective value.
Ch. 7
Methodenstreit
1883–84 dispute between Menger (deduction) and the Historical School (history).
Ch. 5
Monetarism
Friedman: money matters, inflation is a monetary phenomenon.
Ch. 11
Multiplier
k = 1/(1 − c): a spending rise leads to a larger rise in income.
Ch. 9
Mundell triangle
Fixed rate, free capital and independent monetary policy cannot coexist.
Ch. 9
NAIRU
Unemployment rate below which inflation accelerates.
Ch. 11
Natural price
Price around which market prices gravitate.
Ch. 4
Neoclassical synthesis
Samuelson: Keynes in the short run, neoclassical equilibrium in the long run.
Ch. 10
Net product
Physiocrats: the surplus of agriculture over what is consumed in producing it.
Ch. 3
Oikonomia
Household management (Xenophon, Aristotle); the root of “economics”.
Ch. 1
Organic composition of capital
q = Cf / Cv; if it rises with e constant, the profit rate falls.
Ch. 6
Output gap
Actual minus potential output.
Ch. 9
Pareto optimum
No one can gain without someone else losing; silent on distribution.
Ch. 8
Partial equilibrium
Marshall: study one market holding others fixed.
Ch. 8
Permanent income
Friedman: consumption follows lifetime average income.
Ch. 11
Phillips curve
Inverse relation between unemployment and inflation (wages).
Ch. 10
Pigouvian tax
Tax equal to the marginal external cost.
Ch. 8
Poor Laws debate
Malthus: relief increases population; Mill and others defend assistance.
Ch. 5
Price elasticity of demand
ε = (∂Q/Q)/(∂p/p); |ε| > 1 elastic, < 1 inelastic.
Ch. 7
Productive vs sterile classes
Physiocrats: farmers are productive, artisans and merchants sterile.
Ch. 3
Quantity theory of money
Bodin: more money in circulation raises the general price level.
Ch. 2
r > g
Piketty: return on capital exceeds growth, so wealth concentrates.
Ch. 11
Randomised controlled trial
Duflo: random assignment to test a policy against a control group.
Ch. 12
Rate of exploitation
e = Pl / Cv.
Ch. 6
Rational expectations
Agents use all available information (Muth, Lucas).
Ch. 11
Ricardian equivalence
Barro: debt-financed spending is offset by private saving.
Ch. 11
Say’s law
Supply creates its own demand; no lasting general crisis.
Ch. 5
Stagflation
Simultaneous inflation and unemployment (1973–79).
Ch. 10
Stationary state
Ricardo: accumulation stops as diminishing returns raise rent and squeeze profit.
Ch. 4
Stolper–Samuelson effect
A tariff raises the return to the protected factor and lowers the other.
Ch. 10
Surplus value
Marx: Pl, the value produced beyond the wage (Cv).
Ch. 6
Tableau économique
Quesnay (1758): circulation of income between classes.
Ch. 3
Taylor rule
i = 2 + π + 0.5(π − 2) + 0.5ỹ.
Ch. 9
Time inconsistency
Kydland–Prescott: an ex-ante optimal policy is not optimal ex post.
Ch. 11
Trade balance
Exports minus imports; the mercantilist target.
Ch. 2
Usury
Lending at interest, condemned by Aristotle and, in principle, by the scholastics.
Ch. 1
Utilitarianism
Bentham: maximise total utility; limits are interpersonal comparison and distribution.
